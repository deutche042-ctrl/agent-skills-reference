## Shot-Size Selection Rules

### Long Shot
**Purpose**: Reveal the environment, establish the setting, and define spatial relationships.

**Use when**:
- Opening a scene and establishing its location
- Connecting different settings during a transition
- Showing a large-scale event such as a war, parade, or natural disaster
- Contrasting a small character against the environment

**Examples**:
- Opening: A wide view of the marquis's residence conveys the oppressive depth of the compound.
- Transition: Cut from a long shot of the capital to the marquis's residence.
- War: A wide battlefield view reveals the opposing armies.

### Full Shot
**Purpose**: Show a character's full-body posture in relation to the surrounding environment.

**Use when**:
- Introducing a character and displaying the complete design
- Establishing how a character interacts with the setting
- Showing a complete physical action
- Connecting different spaces during a transition

**Examples**:
- Shen Wanyu stands alone in the covered corridor.
- Lu Yanli writhes in pain inside the room, with his full action visible.
- A character crosses the garden as the environment moves around them.

### Medium Shot
**Purpose**: Show a character's upper body; well suited to dialogue and ordinary narration.

**Use when**:
- Showing interaction in dialogue scenes
- Balancing character and environment in ordinary narration
- Showing changes in expression and emotion
- Revealing relationships in multi-character scenes

**Examples**:
- Lu Yanli and Shen Wanyu converse.
- The old marchioness scolds Shen Wanyu.
- Several characters gather together.

### Medium Close-up
**Purpose**: Focus on a character's facial expression and upper body.

**Use when**:
- Communicating an internal emotional state
- Emphasizing important dialogue
- Capturing a subtle reaction
- Showing emotional shifts in psychological moments

**Examples**:
- Shen Wanyu looks troubled.
- Lu Yanli shows pain.
- The old marchioness fixes someone with a stern gaze.

### Close-up
**Purpose**: Emphasize a particular detail or intense emotion.

**Use when**:
- Heightening emotion at a climax
- Emphasizing a key prop or action
- Communicating inner activity through the eyes
- Highlighting a special effect such as system text

**Examples**:
- Shen Wanyu's widened eyes
- Lu Yanli's face twisted in pain
- A close-up of system text
- A hand gripping a skirt or twisting a handkerchief

### Extreme Close-up
**Purpose**: Show a minute detail from extremely close range.

**Use when**:
- Showing pupils change in shock
- Emphasizing tears or sweat
- Highlighting an important prop detail
- Visualizing an extreme internal emotional shift

**Examples**:
- A tear at the corner of Shen Wanyu's eye
- A vein standing out on Lu Yanli's forehead
- Fine detail in system text

## Camera-Movement Selection Rules

### Static Shot
**Characteristic**: The camera remains still.

**Use when**:
- Observing calmly or presenting objectively
- Showing relationships in a stable conversation
- Establishing an environment and its space
- Building suspense while waiting for a sudden event

**Examples**:
- A static medium two-shot during dialogue
- A full-room view that establishes the environment
- The calm before a storm

### Dolly In
**Characteristic**: The camera moves toward the subject.

**Use when**:
- Emphasizing a detail or important element
- Escalating emotion
- Building suspense by revealing the truth gradually
- Directing attention toward a character

**Examples**:
- Move from a medium shot into a close-up of Shen Wanyu's face.
- Move in on the system text.
- Move in on a key prop.

### Dolly Out
**Characteristic**: The camera moves away from the subject.

**Use when**:
- Revealing the environment or releasing tension
- Connecting settings in a transition
- Showing a character's isolation
- Allowing emotion to subside

**Examples**:
- Pull back from Shen Wanyu's face to reveal the room.
- Pull back from a close-up to a long shot.
- Pull away from the character to reveal the environment.

### Pan
**Characteristic**: The camera rotates horizontally.

**Use when**:
- Following a moving character
- Scanning and revealing an environment
- Showing relationships between characters
- Creating a smooth transition

**Examples**:
- Pan with Shen Wanyu as she walks through the covered corridor.
- Scan the room and its furnishings.
- Pan between two speakers.

### Tilt
**Characteristic**: The camera rotates vertically.

**Use when**:
- Revealing differences in height
- Looking down from above
- Looking up from below
- Showing the scale of architecture

**Examples**:
- Look down from the ceiling at Lu Yanli in pain.
- Tilt up from Shen Wanyu's feet to her face.
- Reveal the grandeur of the residence's architecture.

### Handheld Shot
**Characteristic**: Simulates the shake of a handheld camera.

**Use when**:
- Expressing chaos or loss of control
- Increasing immersion through subjective viewpoint
- Creating horror and tension
- Adding energy to an action scene

**Examples**:
- Shen Wanyu's viewpoint when startled
- Lu Yanli struggling in agony
- A chaotic fight

### Tracking Shot
**Characteristic**: The camera follows a moving subject.

**Use when**:
- Showing a character's movement through space
- Following the protagonist to increase immersion
- Transitioning smoothly between settings
- Showing dynamic action

**Examples**:
- Follow Shen Wanyu through the covered corridor.
- Follow a running character.
- Follow a character into a room.

### Orbit Shot
**Characteristic**: The camera circles the subject.

**Use when**:
- Creating drama and impact
- Revealing a 360-degree environment
- Showing relationships and interactions
- Intensifying emotion and atmosphere

**Examples**:
- Circle Shen Wanyu to emphasize her isolation.
- Orbit to reveal the room.
- Circle a key prop.

### Dolly Zoom
**Characteristic**: The camera moves while the lens zooms, changing perspective.

**Use when**:
- Creating dramatic visual impact
- Expressing a sudden emotional shift or unease
- Highlighting an anomaly and building suspense
- Creating dream or hallucination effects

**Examples**:
- The instant Shen Wanyu realizes the system exists
- The visual shock when Lu Yanli's lie becomes reality
- Alternating between memory and reality

## Shot Combinations for Common Scenes

### Opening Scene
**Combination**: Static long shot → dolly-in medium shot → panning medium shot

**Effect**:
1. Establish the environment and spatial relationships.
2. Move toward the subject to direct attention.
3. Pan with the character to draw the viewer into the story.

**Example**:
- Static long shot of the marquis's residence → move in toward Shen Wanyu in the covered corridor → pan with her toward the wedding chamber

### Dialogue Scene
**Combination**: Static medium shot → cut to medium close-up → pull back from close-up

**Effect**:
1. Establish the full conversation.
2. Focus on the speaker.
3. Reveal the listener's reaction.

**Example**:
- Lu Yanli and Shen Wanyu in a medium two-shot → Lu Yanli speaks in medium close-up → pull back from Shen Wanyu's close-up reaction

### Emotional Outburst
**Combination**: Sudden medium close-up → handheld shake → static close-up

**Effect**:
1. A sudden medium close-up marks the triggering event.
2. Handheld movement creates chaos.
3. A static close-up concentrates on the character's emotion.

**Example**:
- Lu Yanli suddenly screams → he writhes on the bed in a handheld shot → static close-up of his anguished expression

### Transition Scene
**Combination**: Static full shot → dolly-in medium shot → dolly-out medium shot

**Effect**:
1. Establish the environment.
2. Move in to guide the transition.
3. Pull back to release tension and enter the new scene.

**Example**:
- Full view of the old marchioness's room → move toward the doorway → pull back as Shen Wanyu leaves

### Suspense Scene
**Combination**: Dolly-in close-up → high-angle medium close-up → panning medium shot

**Effect**:
1. The advancing close-up builds suspense.
2. The high angle conveys authority or oppression.
3. The pan releases tension.

**Example**:
- Close-up of system text → the old marchioness looking down at Shen Wanyu → pan across the room

### Flashback Scene
**Combination**: Static medium shot → dolly-in close-up → dolly out from close-up with a filter

**Effect**:
1. Establish the remembered setting.
2. Focus on the decisive moment.
3. Release emotion and return to reality with a filter.

**Example**:
- Medium shot of the Shen residence garden → close-up of the warm jade → pull back with a warm-toned filter

## Techniques for Avoiding Repetition

### Vary Shot Sizes
**Technique**: Use a different shot size in adjacent shots.

**Rules**:
- Do not use two consecutive medium shots.
- Do not follow a close-up immediately with another close-up; transition through a medium shot or medium close-up.
- Alternate long shots and close-ups to create rhythm.

**Example**:
- Incorrect: medium → medium → medium
- Correct: medium → medium close-up → close-up → medium → full

### Vary Camera Movement
**Technique**: Use diverse movement to avoid monotony.

**Rules**:
- Do not make every shot static.
- Do not use the same movement more than three times in a row.
- Vary movement speed.

**Example**:
- Incorrect: static → static → static
- Correct: static → dolly in → pan → handheld → dolly out

### Vary Duration
**Technique**: Adjust duration to content density.

**Rules**:
- Give important content slightly longer, 2–3 seconds.
- Use shorter durations, 1–1.5 seconds, for fast-paced scenes.
- Keep transitions moderate, around 2 seconds.
- Do not give every shot the same duration.

**Example**:
- Incorrect: 1s → 1s → 1s → 1s
- Correct: 2s → 1.5s → 3s → 1s → 2s

### Combine Shot Size and Movement
**Technique**: Coordinate shot size and movement to create a varied visual rhythm.

**Rules**:
- Medium + static: stable dialogue
- Close-up + dolly in: emotional escalation
- Medium close-up + handheld: chaos
- Full + dolly out: transition

**Example**:
- Shot 1: static medium shot for dialogue
- Shot 2: dolly-in close-up for emotional escalation
- Shot 3: handheld medium close-up for chaos
- Shot 4: dolly-out full shot for transition
- Shot 5: panning medium shot to scan the environment

### Create Progression Across Time Ranges
**Technique**: The three time ranges within one storyboard segment must progress logically.

**Rules**:
- 0–3 seconds: Establish the scene or begin the event.
- 3–7 seconds: Develop the event or escalate emotion.
- 7–10 seconds: Show the result or release tension.

**Example**:
- 0–3 seconds: Lu Yanli speaks in a static medium shot.
- 3–7 seconds: Dolly in to Shen Wanyu's close-up reaction.
- 7–10 seconds: System text appears as the camera pulls back from a close-up.

## Summary

Following these rules produces varied, rhythmic storyboards:

1. **Varied shot sizes**: Include at least five—long, full, medium, medium close-up, and close-up.
2. **Varied camera movements**: Include at least five—static, dolly in, dolly out, pan, and cut.
3. **Varied durations**: Adjust each shot to its content, from one to three seconds.
4. **Varied combinations**: Pair shot sizes and camera movements appropriately.
5. **Logical progression**: Make the three time ranges within each storyboard segment progress logically.

Avoid making every shot a “static medium shot,” and avoid using identical durations throughout. These are essential requirements for a high-quality storyboard.
